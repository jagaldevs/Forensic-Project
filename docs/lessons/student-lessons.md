# Digital Forensics: student lesson sheets

Year 8 Science · HRS Digital Forensics Lab · one sheet per lesson.

**In every case, ask three questions about each piece of evidence:**

1. What does this evidence **show**?
2. What does it **not** show?
3. What **further evidence** would help me decide?

Your progress is saved on the device you use. If Mrs Staggs asks, copy your **progress code**
(top bar → Back up progress) into your notes at the end of each lesson.

## Lesson 1: Is this proof?

**Today I'm learning** the difference between what evidence shows (an observation) and what we
work out from it (an inference).

**I can:**

- sort statements into observations and inferences
- say one thing a photo's metadata shows, and one thing it does not show.

**What to do**

1. With a partner, sort the cards into **Observation** and **Inference**. Be ready to explain
   one that was hard to decide.
2. Open the lab at **forensic-project.netlify.app**. Choose a **codename**, not your real name.
3. Open the **Training Academy**. Complete _Digital Footprints_, then _Metadata Matters_.
4. Finished early? Try _IP Addresses & Networks_.

**Think about it:** a photo says it was taken at 8:01 pm. Whose clock says that? Could it be
wrong?

**Exit ticket**

An observation from today: ____________________________________________________________

An inference someone might make from it: ________________________________________________

## Lesson 2: How sure are we?

**Today I'm learning** that measurements have uncertainty, and that this limits what we can
conclude.

**I can:**

- explain what an "accuracy radius" means
- use evidence to judge a claim, and say what the evidence does **not** show.

**What to do**

1. Starter: a map says your friend is "within 25 km of the city". Where could they be?
2. Training Academy: complete _IP Addresses & Networks_.
3. Open **The Waterfront Alibi**. Read the briefing from Detective Sergeant Tran.
4. Choose your **support level**: Guided, Standard or Deep dive. You get the same questions at
   every level, and you can change it at any time from the top bar.
5. Investigate: run the IP lookup, apply for warrants carefully (you only have two), pin
   evidence to the board and link it to the claims.
6. Make a judgement for each question and press **Check**. Read the hints and try again.

**Think about it:** the IP lookup says "Darwin". How big is "Darwin" when the accuracy radius is
25 km?

**Exit ticket**

The IP lookup shows ______________________________________________________________

but it does **not** show _________________________________________________________

---

## Lesson 3: Limits, ethics and warrants

**Today I'm learning** that evidence has limits, and that collecting people's data needs a good,
fair reason.

**I can:**

- explain why a device is not the same as a person
- decide whether a request for data is fair (proportionate), and give a reason.

**What to do**

1. Class debrief: compare your verdicts for The Waterfront Alibi with the answer key.
2. Training Academy: complete _Ethics & Privacy_.
3. Warrant debate: for each scenario card, decide:
   - Is it **necessary**? (Does it help answer the question?)
   - Is it **proportionate**? (Is it no bigger than it needs to be?)
   - What is the **least intrusive** way to find out?
4. Finish question 4 of The Waterfront Alibi, then open the **Report** from the dock.

**Think about it:** Riley's laptop was online all evening. Does that prove Riley was home?

**Exit ticket**

A request I would approve, and why: ____________________________________________________

A request I would refuse, and why: _____________________________________________________

---

## Lesson 4: When clocks disagree

**Today I'm learning** that measuring instruments, including clocks, can have errors, and that
scientists correct for them before comparing data.

**I can:**

- explain the difference between a systematic error and a random error
- correct a device's clock on a timeline and explain why.

**What to do**

1. Starter: two clocks, one 30 minutes ahead, one a few seconds off. Which error can you fix?
2. Training Academy: complete _Timestamps & Time Zones_.
3. Open **Where was Jamie?** Jamie is safe. Your job: work out why the search went to the
   wrong place.
4. Use Messages, Social Feed, CCTV and the Map. Watch for new evidence arriving.
5. Open the **Timeline**. If two events seem impossible together, check every device's clock
   before deciding someone is wrong.

**Think about it:** Darwin is UTC+9:30 all year. What if a phone thinks it is somewhere else?

**Exit ticket**

The device with the wrong clock was _______________ . I know because ___________________

I corrected it by _______________________________________________________________

---

## Lesson 5: New evidence changes the story

**Today I'm learning** how new evidence can change a conclusion, and how feedback from other
investigators improves our reasoning.

**I can:**

- explain how one piece of evidence changed my thinking
- give useful feedback on someone else's evidence board.

**What to do**

1. Finish **Where was Jamie?** For question 3, use the Map's travel calculator. For question 4,
   use your Timeline.
2. Gallery walk: leave your Evidence Board open. Visit others and leave sticky notes:
   - **green**: a strong link ("this supports the claim because…")
   - **yellow**: a question ("what does this _not_ show?")
3. Class debrief: why did the search start at Mindil Beach?

**Think about it:** can you think of a time new evidence changed what scientists believed?

**Exit ticket**

One piece of evidence that changed my thinking: ________________________________________

How it changed my thinking: _____________________________________________________

## Lesson 6: Whose search was it?

**Today I'm learning** to use observation and inference with a phone's search history and with
sensors, and to work more on my own before the assessment.

**I can:**

- explain why a search in a history does not show who typed it
- describe what a fitness band and a motion light measure, and what they cannot tell us
- decide whether a request for someone's data is fair.

**What to do**

1. Training Academy: complete _Search History & Cookies_.
2. Open **The Midnight Raid**. Read the briefing from Mr Pritchard and choose your support level.
3. Work as if it were the assessment: investigate carefully, and only press **Check** when you
   are confident.
4. Some data belongs to other people: you will need to **ask permission**. Ask only for what
   the question needs.
5. Watch for new evidence arriving.

**Think about it:** a fitness band says someone was "asleep". Did it see them sleeping?

**Exit ticket**

The search history shows ____________________________________________________________

but it does **not** show _________________________________________________________
